## §0 · THE CONSISTENCY GATE (mandatory, before ANY new element)

Before designing or building any boss, biome, wizard, potion, enemy, NPC, prop, or
architectural style — answer all eight in one line each, then log the element to
`docs/CANON_LOG.md`. A weak answer means iterate on paper; do not build.

1. **LAW** — How does it serve sleep, or threaten waking? (If neither, it does not exist in this world.)
2. **SCENE** — Which chapter does it live in, and does it obey that chapter's locked accent hue?
3. **SILHOUETTE** — Describe it as a pure black shape. Is it readable and unique at 64px?
4. **KEEPER** — Whose work, light, or care is it? (Everything here is kept by someone, or was.)
5. **WEAR** — What did centuries of use leave on it? (Nothing important is new. Nothing important is clean.)
6. **INVERSION** — Name the generic-fantasy default it refuses or inverts.
7. **WHISPER** — Can a player understand it with zero text? What question does it make them ask?
8. **COVER** — Describe the one frame of it worth painting. If you can't, it isn't done.

**Scope rider:** does BRIEF.md §7.9 allow building it in the current phase? If not, design
it on paper, mark it `POST-V1` in the canon log, and stop there.

**THE BANNED LIST (standing, never re-litigate):** neon cyan holo / scanlines / mono
microtype / cursive gold / cluttered HUD / template feel (Ian's absolute bans) · bull-man-
with-axe-in-a-maze minotaur · taverns, inns, merchants, gold coins · XP numbers, damage
numbers, minimaps, lore books, readable in-world text · glowing runes, purple "magic
energy", corruption goo · skeleton/zombie/goblin mobs · lava level, ice-palace level ·
chosen-one prophecy · anything explained in a paragraph.

## §1 · THE ONE LAW (cosmology — never stated in-game, ever)

**This world is the dream of something vast that fell asleep before history began.
Waking is the only apocalypse.**

Everything derives from this:

- **The red sun is not a sun.** It is the Ember — the place where the Dreamer's heart
  presses against the sky's thin skin. It never moves. It *breathes* (a ~8-second pulse,
  barely perceptible — post-Gate visual hook). Its color is the world's blood-hue: what
  the dream fears or loves most, it paints in the Ember's red.
- **Death is not feared here.** The dead simply sink deeper into the dream. What is
  feared is *noise, haste, and want* — the three wakeful things.
- **The people learned the Law not as revelation but as craft:** things done quietly
  lasted; things done loudly frayed the world at the edges. Over centuries their whole
  civilization reorganized around one duty — keep the Dreamer asleep (§2).
- **Wake-scars:** recently the dream has begun to thin. Meadows of white flowers appear
  overnight where the Dreamer almost surfaced. In the largest of them, two knights
  opened their eyes. They are the first fully awake things in living memory. The world
  noticed. That is why it whispers: *"What will you do?"*
- **The knights** (per BRIEF §7.2, the slides' hook — went to sleep, woke here): they are
  sleepers from the other side who fell in. The keepers can tell. It is why they kneel
  to the knights AND fear them: an awake thing is a hole the morning can pour through.
  This is never texted. Two candidate truths exist (fell in from outside / dreamed-up
  defenders); the game commits to neither on screen.
- **The ending is the Law's proof:** the journey ends where dreams do — waking. The
  knights wake OUT ("And waking up"). The world remains asleep. One more window is lit.
- **Protected mystery:** the floating tree under the pink moon (the epilogue image) is
  NEVER explained. Every mythology needs one image it refuses to open. This is ours.

**The north-star emotion** (every element is tuned to produce it): *hushed awe —
protective tenderness inside enormity.* Not conquest. Not power. The feeling of guarding
something enormous and asleep.

## §2 · THE TEN LAWS (visual + design DNA — the frame-side of §1)

1. **One accent hue per scene** (locked in BRIEF §7.3); everything else crushed
   near-black + muted earth. The accent is emotional, never decorative.
2. **The world is huge; the knights are small.** ≤1/9 frame height in play,
   ≤1/20 in hero vistas. Scale contrast = awe.
3. **Every silhouette must read as a black shape at 64px** — bosses, buildings, props,
   NPCs. If the silhouette needs color to read, rebuild it. (Doré test: every
   composition must survive in pure value before hue is allowed.)
4. **Layer after layer of distance, glowing haze between every band.** No black-on-black
   stacking, ever (proven law — Gate 0 iteration 3).
5. **Nothing moves fast except danger.** All ambient motion is drift: clouds, petals,
   moths, breath. Speed is the vocabulary of threat.
6. **Every light implies a keeper.** No unmotivated light sources. A lit window is a
   person. A candle is a duty. Darkness is nobody's fault — light is somebody's work.
7. **No readable text in-world.** Tally-marks, gesture, iconography only. The serif
   whisper (2–5 words) is the only sentence-maker in the universe.
8. **Sound is sacred.** The mix whispers so that one toll can matter. Loud = event.
   Silence is scored, not empty.
9. **Everything answers "how did this help someone sleep?"** — architecture, tools,
   ritual, clothing. If it has no answer, it belongs to the dead Loud Age stratum
   (§3) or it doesn't exist.
10. **The painted medium is canon.** Grain + glaze + lifted blacks on every frame; visible
    brushwork; soft edges; unfinished edges at frame borders (Alan Lee law: the eye
    completes ruins better than the brush). Every frame reads as scanned from a 1978
    paperback. The grain launders all synthetic slickness — it is load-bearing.

**The art-canon these laws were extracted from** (full study:
`ObsidianPKM/research/somnium-art-canon-2026-07-12.md`): Frazetta (paint the energy, not
the anatomy — detail only at the focal strike) · McBride (believability comes from wear;
every object owned by someone poor) · Nasmith (buildings look like the mountain agreed
to them) · Alan Lee (ancientness through softness) · Friedrich (the Rückenfigur — frame
the knights from behind, contemplating, never conquering: THE co-op camera grammar) ·
Doré (value-first composition, scale via cascading recession) · Blanche (sacred things
accumulate crust — votive wax, seals, garlands; the holy and the rotten fused) · Elden
Ring (awe = beauty + wrongness at scale) · SotC (killing the sublime must cost the
player) · Journey (constrain communication until presence itself is emotional) · ICO
(one continuous act of care can carry a game) · Berserk (earn the wide shot — spectacle
lands only after intimacy) · Mononoke (the world is not FOR the player) · paperback
covers (every scene needs its cover painting — the wallpaper moment is the unit of design).

## §3 · THE HUSH (the one civilization)

Not a kingdom — a **discipline**. The Hush is what humanity became once it learned the
One Law. Its members are **keepers**. Its great works are instruments of lullaby at
landscape scale.

### History as strata (environmental layers — the player reads these, never a date)
1. **The Loud Age** — before the Law. Towers with battlements, war-banners, bells rung
   freely. Always found as the deepest ruin layer: broken, overgrown, half-swallowed.
   *This is where generic medieval fantasy lives in our world: as the dead thing under
   it.* (The moonlit castle silhouette of Scene 2 is Loud Age. The shade-knights are its
   nightmares, §9.)
2. **The Learning** — first wayshrines, first tally-stones, first stilled bell.
3. **The High Hush** — the great works: cradle-architecture, wind-organs tuned to hum in
   storms, rivers rerouted to murmur, the Bellbinding (every bell in the world filled
   with wax or wrapped — §7). The Black Tower's Great Bell, too large to fill, was
   unbound instead: its clapper removed (§5).
4. **The Lying Down** — the masterstroke and the heartbreak: sleep thickens the dream,
   so the Hush began *joining* it. Whole towns lay down together. Not death — beds,
   dust like snow, grass growing through floorboards around untouched sleepers.
5. **Now** — a handful of keepers remain awake, tending lights and hushes. Wake-scars
   spread. Two knights are walking east.

### Architecture DNA
- Everything is **furniture for sleep at civilizational scale**. Every building reads,
  in silhouette, as one of three shapes: **a hood, a cradle, or a kneeling figure.**
- **Muffled geometry:** cloth-wrapped clappers, moss-padded thresholds, wax-sealed
  joints, straw-cushioned streets. No striking surface left bare.
- **Cradle-curves:** parapets curve inward like bed-rails. No battlements (battlements
  are Loud Age — instantly reads as "older, dead layer").
- **Processional axes:** all old roads run toward the Ember. Mile-monoliths mark watches,
  not miles.
- **Materials:** soot-blackened timber, wax-sealed stone, wrapped bronze, bone-ivory
  plaster, felt and wool everywhere. Tarnish, never shine.
- **Iconography (no text, ever):** the **closed eye** (every crescent in this world is an
  eyelid, not a moon) · **tally-marks** = watches kept (the Scene-1 wayshrine's carved
  groove is one) · the **spiral** = descent into sleep (the Gothic Stair's plan is a
  spiral) · knots = kept promises.
- **The gesture language** (keepers do not speak; the future emote set): palm-down =
  *quiet/stay* · palm to chest = *I keep you* · two fingers to the eyes = *watch* ·
  cupped hands = *carry the light / share warmth*.

### What already exists, canonized (retrofits — the current build now MEANS something)
- The **white flower patch** the knights wake in = a wake-scar (§1). Locked.
- The **thorn sentinel tree** (S1 west edge) = dream scar-tissue grown over an old wound.
- The **wayshrine stone** = a lullaby-station; its groove is a keeper's tally.
- The **ruined tower's two warm windows** = a keeper still on watch. Every kept light in
  the game implies its keeper (Law 6).
- The **ravens** = sound-eaters. Keepers feed them noise (the Ferrier keeps shouts in
  jars for them, §7). They are the only things permitted to be loud, briefly.
- The **fireflies** = lamp-sparks that escaped and went feral. Harmless. Beloved.
- The **distant earth-tolls** (to be added post-Gate, §11) = the Minotaur, counting
  watches, five scenes away.

## §4 · THE FIVE CHAPTERS (v1 scenes — accent hues locked by BRIEF §7.3)

Each chapter = one accent hue, one weather, one emotion, one cover painting, one co-op
beat, and questions it must make the player ask.

### Chapter 1 — RED-SUN FIELD *(blood-red sun / black mountains / white flowers)*
- **What it is:** the largest wake-scar in the world. Flat, endless, watched.
- **Weather:** dead-still air; ash-petals falling off the Ember (never rain here).
- **Architecture:** Learning-era — wayshrine, mile-monolith, the watching ruined tower.
- **Flora/fauna:** wake-daisies, thorn sentinels, ravens, feral fireflies.
- **Mythology:** keepers avoid this field. Flowers where nothing was planted mean the
  Dreamer stirred here. The knights waking IN it confirms every fear.
- **Music/light:** wind + one bowed-glass note, held. The Ember low and enormous.
- **Emotion:** lonely awe. *Something is wrong with how beautiful this is.*
- **Cover painting:** knight rising from flattened flowers, red disc filling the sky.
- **Co-op beat:** P1 wakes alone. The field is deliberately too big for one person.
- **Must-ask questions:** why do the flowers grow in a circle? who keeps that far light?

### Chapter 2 — CAMPFIRE NIGHT *(ember red in blue-green dark)*
- **What it is:** the Pilgrim Road — the High Hush's processional way, mile-monoliths
  marching toward the Ember. A Loud Age castle silhouette on the moonlit ridge: the
  old dead world watching the road that replaced it.
- **Weather:** wind that carries humming from very far away.
- **Fauna:** distant keeper processions passing as lantern-lines on the horizon (never
  reachable — staging, not NPCs).
- **Mythology:** the road was walked as liturgy: processions carrying stilled bells,
  singing the Dreamer down. The campfire circles beside it are rest-stations with
  fire-cradles worn by ten thousand kettles.
- **Music/light:** fire crackle + the lullaby hummed by unseen distant walkers (§11).
- **Emotion:** companionship against enormity. The warmest frame in the game.
- **Cover painting:** two knights at the ember fire, monolith row and dead castle behind.
- **Co-op beat:** **P2's canonical wake-in** (BRIEF-locked): the fire is fed by both —
  resting together is how the world re-dreams the scene forward (shared sleep = scene
  transition = drop-in point, all one system).
- **Must-ask:** who is humming? why do the monoliths lean toward the sun?

### Chapter 3 — BLUE-HOUR VILLAGE *(orange windows & fields on ultramarine night)*
- **What it is:** a Lying-Down town — everyone asleep for a century, safe in their beds,
  and the town still *kept*: lamps lit, thresholds swept, moth-lofts humming. The
  biggest vista in the game (BRIEF: the beauty beat).
- **Architecture:** houses built AROUND their beds; windows shaped to show sleepers are
  safe; paper-and-horn lamp lofts (the Moth Warden's quarter, §7).
- **Flora/fauna:** lamp-moths in slow orbits; roosting sound-eater ravens; window-boxes
  gone feral with wake-daisies (a warning sign the Warden prunes nightly).
- **Mythology:** the town lay down together on one agreed night. The Warden was chosen
  by lot to stay awake and keep them. She is still keeping them.
- **Music/light:** one detuned music-box heard from one window; her orange lamps ARE the
  scene's accent, floating on ultramarine.
- **Emotion:** tenderness + trespass. You are walking through someone's kept promise.
- **Cover painting:** street of orange windows, each framing a sleeper; two knights tiny
  between them; moths circling the one lamp in the foreground.
- **Co-op beat:** muffled doors (synchronized slow-open or they groan — §10); optional
  tuck-in acts; the Warden gestures the knights onward (silent, gesture language).
- **Must-ask:** are they dead? (No — and realizing *no* is the scene's gut-punch.)

### Chapter 4 — GOTHIC STAIR *(blood/magenta sky on black stone)*
- **What it is:** the spiral descent-and-climb of the High Hush's greatest works — and
  its two great failures. Wax-fall architecture (the Tallowwright's centuries of
  re-pouring), the Bellbinder's workshop, ranks of kneeling keeper statues — *some of
  which are not statues* (asleep standing; and see §5 post-fight for what the rest are).
  The waterfall chasm ROARS — the one loud place in the world, the Ferrier's one
  failure; his weirs and barge-mill toil below, eternally ashamed.
- **Weather:** updraft, spray, bats (§9) riding the roar.
- **Music/light:** near-silence between roar-pockets — drips, breath, rests (the
  Bellbinder's leitmotif is rests). Magenta sky through slit windows.
- **Emotion:** dread + reverence, tightening.
- **Cover painting:** the spiral stair hugging the chasm wall, ivory wax-falls, two tiny
  knights, bats crossing the magenta slit of sky.
- **Co-op beat:** the roar makes gesture the ONLY language (mechanically: audio cues
  masked, visual telegraphs only — co-op learns to read each other).
- **Must-ask:** why is every statue kneeling? why are all the bell molds filled?

### Chapter 5 — BLACK TOWER *(red beast in blue-white snow)* → §5 THE FIRST AWAKE.

### Epilogue — the floating tree, the pink moon, "And waking up." Protected mystery (§1).
The lullaby resolves — the only resolved cadence in the entire game (§11). Cut to title.

## §5 · THE MINOTAUR — "the first awake" (v1 finale, BRIEF-locked silhouette)

*Locked by BRIEF/slide: huge red-furred minotaur with a greatsword, black tower, tiny
knights below, blue-white snow, 60%+ screen height, 2–3 phases, ground shockwaves.
The bible's job: make that exact image inevitable in THIS world.*

- **Origin:** four hundred years ago the first wake-scar opened in a belfry town. One
  keeper woke and could not re-sleep. Wakefulness grows in a dreamed world: the dream
  pushed against him, and what the dream fears it paints in the Ember's red — he grew
  vast, horned, pelted in the heart's own color. A nightmare shape around a keeper's soul.
- **History:** he asked the Bellbinder for a task instead of a mercy. The Great Bell of
  the Black Tower was too large to fill with wax — so she unbound it: took its clapper
  and gave it to him. **His greatsword is the clapper of the Great Bell.** He carries
  the loudest voice in the world so that it can never speak. He has walked the tower's
  snowfield ever since, striking the hours against the stone — the distant earth-tolls
  heard faintly in every chapter (§11). Counting watches no one asked him to keep.
- **Why snow:** the tower stands where the dream runs cold and thin — high dream-altitude.
  The quietest place in the world guards the loudest object in it.
- **Silhouette:** mountain-shouldered hunch; horns sweep DOWN (a bull's threat display
  inverted — he lowers his head to listen, not to charge); the clapper-sword drags a
  trench in the snow. Readable at 64px by the hunch + down-horns + dragged line.
- **Concept-art direction:** Frazetta mass, McBride harness realism (keeper's cording,
  patched straps), Blanche crust: **generations of keepers anointed him** — dripped
  votive wax down his shoulders like armor, flower garlands (centuries old, gone to
  seed) wound on his wrists. They loved him. It shows. It has to show.
- **Color language:** the scene's locked accent — his red pelt is the only warm mass in
  blue-white. Rim-lit by the Ember behind snowfall.
- **Arena:** the snowfield at the tower's foot. Above him, in the black belfry mouth,
  **the Great Bell still hangs — mute, clapperless.** Buried under the snow: the bronze
  acoustic floor the belfry once stood on — hits *toll dully underfoot*.
- **Reveal sequence:** he is first seen kneeling, snow-drifted, indistinguishable from a
  colossal statue — the players' own footsteps toll the buried floor — his ear moves
  first. Then he stands, and stands, *and stands.* No roar. He points the clapper at
  them: palm-down with the off-hand. *Quiet. Stay.* (Gesture language — he is still a
  keeper.) They came anyway. The fight is his grief about that.
- **Camera:** locked wide (both knights + his full height). Pushes in only twice: the
  hour-toll mid-fight, and the death kneel. (Berserk law: earn the close-up.)
- **Music:** the lullaby inverted into a war-drum; his strikes land ON the grid — the
  fight has a tempo the players can learn (§11). Phase 3 strips to a single hummed
  voice + the Ember's sub-bass pulse.
- **Combat philosophy & phases (design intent — built in Phase 3, not before):**
  - *Phase 1 — The Watch:* deliberate warning arcs, ground shockwaves (clapper strike =
    ring of snow-bloom), the mid-fight hour-toll he refuses to skip even in battle.
  - *Phase 2 — The Fear:* the dream's terror floods him; red deepens, snow whips
    horizontal, down-horns finally lead. His bellow is the lullaby's first note, *wrong*.
  - *Phase 3 — The Memory:* he fights like the man he was — a keeper's staff-drill
    footwork — and **if a knight falls, he waits.** He will not strike the down. The
    couch goes silent here. This is the ten-year detail.
- **Idle behavior:** if players retreat, he re-kneels facing them and resumes the watch.
  He never pursues beyond the buried floor's edge. He is not a hunter. He is a door.
- **Environmental storytelling:** wax strata on his shoulders (whose? — the Tallowwright's
  ancestors'); garlands on his wrists; a ring of offering-bowls at the arena edge, snow-
  filled; the trench his dragged clapper has carved over four hundred years — a perfect
  circle around the tower.
- **Death sequence:** no ragdoll. He plants the clapper upright in the snow, kneels
  against it — the same pose as the Gothic Stair statues — and greys, snow settling on
  him first. **The stair statues were never statues. Every one is a keeper who finished
  their watch.** The whisper: *"the watch is kept."*
- **Post-fight transformation:** wind moves the Great Bell above — it swings, **mute**.
  Across the vista behind, every window-light the players have seen (and any they relit,
  §10) flickers once, in acknowledgment. The path to the floating tree opens.
- **Player emotion arc:** dread → rhythm-mastery → guilt → tenderness. The must-beat
  (§12): the players hesitate before the final blow with zero text telling them to.
- **Wallpaper moments:** the stand-up reveal · the hour-toll mid-fight (fight frozen,
  both knights braced, clapper against stone) · the mute swinging bell over the kneeling
  grey colossus.

**Critique (the three tests):** Remembered in ten years? — the sealed voice + he-waits-
when-you're-down + statues-were-keepers reveal: yes, they compound. Would painters fight
to paint it? — the reveal frame and the mute bell frame, yes. Only works in THIS world?
— every trait derives from the One Law (clapper, wax crust, kneel-death, hour-toll);
transplanted anywhere else he stops making sense. **Flagged risk:** "he waits" could read
as easy → it exists only in Phase 3, after two phases of lethal pressure, and it makes
the kill *harder emotionally*, not mechanically.

### Post-v1 boss seeds (paper only — each must re-pass the Gate before build)
- **THE UNLULLED CHOIR** *(seed, promising):* a nightmare that leaked from a wake-scar
  and became a congregation of one — a processional mass of joined sleepwalkers singing
  the lullaby *wrong*. Fought by out-singing it (co-op hum-mirroring). Critique: audio
  identity superb; silhouette not yet unique — **parked until the silhouette earns it.**
- **THE FERRIER'S DEBT** *(seed, rejected-until):* the chasm-roar given a body; a flood
  that walks upstream. Critique: currently collapses into "water elemental" — generic.
  **Rejected until it can't be described in two words.**

## §6 · THE LAST KEEPERS (the "wizards" — silent, gesture-only, no dialog trees)

No spellcasters. Magic in this world is *craft so old and patient it looks like magic* —
each keeper maintains one instrument of the civilization-scale lullaby. Each has their
own architecture, palette, potion style, philosophy, soundtrack, and wrongness. All are
**post-v1 as characters** except where their WORK is v1 staging (noted).

### THE TALLOWWRIGHT — keeper of vigil-lights
- **Philosophy:** "A flame is a small awake thing. Teach it manners."
- **Architecture/lab:** wax-fall towers re-poured nightly; a foundry-chapel where candle
  stubs from every kept window are remelted — the same wax, centuries old. The wax
  remembers every window it kept.
- **Palette:** ivory / amber / soot. **Sound:** dripping percussion, slow hot hum.
- **Potion style:** candles that burn liquid light; wax seals that hold a room's warmth.
- **Habit (the wrongness):** carries his own funeral candle, lit. When it gutters he
  trims his remaining years shorter, without complaint.
- **V1 presence:** the Gothic Stair's wax-falls and votive crusts are his family's work.

### THE MOTH WARDEN — keeper of the village lamps
- **Philosophy:** "Light that flutters soothes. Light that stares wakes."
- **Architecture/lab:** paper-and-horn lamp lofts; a dovecote of chrysalises. She dims a
  town the way you tuck in a child. Scene 3's orange windows are HER design.
- **Palette:** moth-grey / lamp-orange. **Sound:** paper flutter, one detuned music-box.
- **Potion style:** moth-dust nocturnes — wing-dust that settles into constellations.
- **Habit:** whispers only to lamps, never to people; wears a sleeping mask pushed up on
  her forehead — ready, the moment her lamps outlive her, to lie down too.
- **V1 presence:** Scene 3 entire; she appears once, in a window, and gestures: *watch.*

### THE FERRIER OF HUSHES — keeper of the waters
- **Philosophy:** "A river is the world talking in its sleep. Keep it mumbling."
- **Architecture/lab:** weirs, braided murmur-channels, a barge-mill that never stops
  turning. He has not stepped on land in decades.
- **Palette:** peat-brown / moon-silver. **Sound:** wheel-creak, water drone.
- **Potion style:** stoppered murmurs — bottles of the river remembering.
- **Habit:** collects shouts in jars and feeds them to the sound-eater ravens.
- **The shame:** the Gothic Stair chasm ROARS — his one failure; he poles beneath it
  forever, unable to hush it, unable to leave it.
- **V1 presence:** the chasm's weirs and his barge silhouette, far below (staging).

### THE BELLBINDER — keeper of voices *(posthumous — v1 environmental storytelling)*
- **Philosophy:** "Every bell is a morning waiting to happen. I bind them so morning
  never comes."
- **The work:** filled every bell in the world with wax. Her workshop on the Gothic
  Stair holds the molds ranked like a choir — every one filled. The Great Bell's mold
  stands empty and tally-less: too large to bind, she unbound it instead, and gave its
  clapper to the one who couldn't sleep (§5).
- **Palette:** bronze / verdigris / candle. **Sound:** none — her leitmotif is rests.
- **Potion style:** one known work: *A Name, Unsaid* (§8).

**Critique:** each keeper = one instrument, one palette, one wrongness; none could exist
outside the One Law; none is "the botanist/astronomer" template. The roster is small on
purpose — four legends beat forty NPCs.

## §8 · KEPT THINGS (alchemy — collectibles as witnesses)

**Rules:** no labels, no menus, no stats (BRIEF non-goal). Each kept thing is one
preserved moment in glass, hung on the belt or shelved at the campfire (the collection
lives where both players rest). Mechanical effect: ambient only — campfire scenes,
whisper variants, small world-acknowledgments. Players keep them because they are
beautiful and because the world clearly once thought they were worth keeping.

| Kept thing | In the glass | Found | Keeper of origin |
|---|---|---|---|
| One Firefly Hour | a single spark orbiting slowly; brightens at dusk | S1 wayshrine | unknown keeper |
| A Held Breath | fog that condenses when danger is near | S2 rest-station | procession-made |
| The Last Warm Window | amber wax + a wick that lights only when both knights sit | S3, the Warden's gift | Tallowwright wax |
| Moth-dust Nocturne | wing-dust settling into slow constellations | S3 lamp-loft | Moth Warden |
| The River Remembering | a murmur; the glass ripples from inside | S4 chasm weir | Ferrier |
| Thunder in Wool | wool-wrapped bottle; glows faintly during storms | S4 stair niche | procession-made |
| A Name, Unsaid | empty. wax-sealed. label turned forever away | S4 Bellbinder's workshop | Bellbinder |
| A Snowfall, Unspent | snow that begins to fall inside only after §5's death | S5 arena edge | the First Awake's |

**Critique:** each passes "players collect them even if they did nothing" (the prompt's
bar); each implies a hand and a reason; none is a potion-bottle-with-HP.

## §9 · THE STIRRED (enemy design language — wraps BRIEF's locked types)

Enemies are **wakefulness leaking** — the dream's old violent memories given ink and
motion. They are not a species. They are symptoms.

- **Shade-knights** *(BRIEF-locked type):* the Loud Age's soldiers, as the dream still
  has nightmares of them — ink-smear silhouettes wearing battlemented armor (the ONLY
  place generic-medieval armor may appear: on the dead layer's ghosts, on purpose).
  They are loud. Their loudness is the horror.
- **Stirred crows** *(BRIEF "crows"):* sound-eater ravens that ate too much scream and
  burst into noise-shapes mid-flight.
- **Stair-bats** *(BRIEF-locked type):* the chasm-roar given wings — the Ferrier's
  failure made flesh; they ride the updraft and dive on anything quieter than they are.
- **Rendering law:** all Stirred read as painted smears — brush-stroke beings, never
  modeled monsters. Skeletons/goblins/zombies remain banned.
- **Combat philosophy:** fights are *accidents of noise* — sparse, placed (BRIEF §7.7),
  and regretted. A killed Stirred doesn't die violently: it **hushes** — deflates into
  settling ink and a held silence.
- **The held-breath rule (ten-year detail):** after every fight the world's ambient bed
  dips to near-silence for two seconds — the world holding its breath — then resumes.
  Players learn to dread the quiet *after* the fight.

## §10 · TWO AWAKE (co-op philosophy)

**Two awake beings in a sleeping world. Every mechanic is a form of keeping:** keep
quiet, keep warm, keep watch, keep each other.

- **The campfire is the master ritual** (S2 template, BRIEF-locked): resting together is
  how the world re-dreams the scene forward — shared sleep = scene transition = P2's
  drop-in fiction = save point. One system, all fiction-native.
- **Muffled doors:** heavy doors open silently only when both knights push slowly, in
  sync. Rushed = a groan that stirs something. Tension without combat.
- **Vigil relighting:** two-knight ritual (one shields the wind, one strikes the spark)
  to relight dead watch-windows/braziers. **Every relit light persists in later vistas**
  — the skyline slowly answers the players' care. (§5's post-fight acknowledgment pays
  this off.)
- **The rope and the toll** (§5 arena): one knight dampens the buried bronze floor with
  the keepers' rope while the other attacks; swap fluidly. The arena's sound is the
  shared mechanic.
- **Revive = embrace** (BRIEF-locked crawl/revive, styled): kneel, cloak over them,
  hold. Warming, not defibrillating.
- **Tuck-in acts (optional tenderness):** cloak a fallen keeper, right a dropped lantern,
  close an open door on a sleeper — small unprompted acts; a distant window lights each
  time. Never scored, never counted.
- **Gesture wheel = the keepers' gesture language** (§3), nothing else. No chat, no
  pings, no emotes-as-jokes.
- **NEVER:** damage numbers, loot rolls, revive HUD timers, competitive anything.

**Critique vs "single player with a friend":** every ritual above is *impossible or
meaningless alone* — the fire needs both, the doors need both, the rope needs both, the
tenderness needs a witness. That's the bar.

## §11 · MUSIC & SOUND (one lullaby, one toll, much silence)

- **THE LULLABY** — the world's leitmotif: five notes, falling contour, ending
  unresolved on the second degree. *The resolution is waking — so it must never
  resolve…* until the epilogue's "And waking up," the game's only resolved cadence.
  Every scene's music is this theme rearranged.
- **THE TOLL** — the Minotaur's earth-strikes, heard from Scene 1 (barely) to Scene 5
  (the fight's own tempo grid). Interval shortens chapter by chapter: the whole game is
  a slow accelerando toward the boss. It is the world's metronome and the players never
  get told what it is until they see him strike the hour mid-fight.
- **Per-scene arrangement:** S1 wind + one bowed-glass note · S2 fire + the lullaby
  hummed by distant unseen walkers · S3 one detuned music-box from one window · S4
  rests, drips, breath, chasm-roar pockets (the Bellbinder's silence) · S5 lullaby as
  war-drum → solo hum + Ember sub-bass pulse (~8s, synced to the sky's breathing) ·
  Epilogue: resolution, first and last.
- **Instrument palette:** bowed glass, wax-muted strings, hand-hum, felt drums,
  sub-bass breath. Nothing brassy, nothing synthetic-bright.
- **Silence law:** silence is scored (the held-breath rule §9; the pre-reveal hush §5).
  The mix whispers so one toll can matter (Law 8). Diegetic first: if a sound has no
  keeper or cause, cut it.
