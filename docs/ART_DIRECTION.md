# ART DIRECTION LAW — Ian's Gate 0 directive (2026-07-12)

**NO enemies. NO mechanics. NO quests. NO UI. NO progression. NO systems.**
One biome only — the **Red-Sun Field** — polished until it is the visual benchmark
for the whole game. It gates everything.

**North star:** not "indie game" — *a forgotten dark fantasy masterpiece*. Every frame
intentionally composed, painted, wallpaper-quality.

**Named failures to kill:** flat terrain · prototype lighting · empty landscape ·
obvious game engine. Wanted instead: ancient dream · painterly composition ·
impossible fantasy · cinematic atmosphere.

**The checklist (from Ian, distilled):**
- TERRAIN: sweeping silhouettes, rolling hills, valleys, rock formations that frame,
  ancient worn paths, broken cliffs — every horizon has shape.
- SKY AS CHARACTER: massive sun (ours is the red sun), moving clouds, drifting fog,
  stars, occasional shooting stars, volumetric haze, subtle gradients.
- LIGHTING: cinematic — strong red keylight, long shadows, deep blacks, never uniform,
  never overexposed.
- DEPTH: layer after layer — foreground grass, midground ruins, background ranges,
  far ranges, atmospheric fog. Feel surrounded by distance.
- VEGETATION: variety — tall grass, flowers, dead branches, moss, ferns, stones;
  nothing repeats obviously.
- ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING: implied history — ruins, collapsed arches, old roads,
  shrines, weathered stones. Lived-in.
- PARALLAX/MOTION: clouds move, fog drifts, foreground sways, particles cross;
  subtle motion everywhere.
- CAMERA: concept-art composition, negative space, player small, world dominates.
- ATMOSPHERE: wind, fireflies, ash/petals, mist, ravens.
- COLOR: one dominant emotional palette — deep black + blood red (+ muted green,
  cold blue only in support). Scene-1 accent stays THE red sun.

**Quality test after every change:** Would someone save this screenshot? Use it as a
wallpaper? Believe it's official concept art from a much larger project?
If no — keep iterating. Stop only when improvements become genuinely hard to find.
